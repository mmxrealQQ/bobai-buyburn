About this test

What does the test measure?

The PHQ-9 records how pronounced depressive symptoms have been over the past two weeks. Its nine questions correspond to the nine symptom criteria for major depression in DSM-5-TR, including low mood, loss of interest, sleep, energy, appetite, self-worth, concentration and thoughts of death.

How is it scored?

Each question is scored from 0 (“Not at all”) to 3 (“Nearly every day”); the total lies between 0 and 27. The bands: 0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–19 moderately severe, 20–27 severe. From 10 points the symptoms are considered clinically relevant (Kroenke et al. 2001). The test also checks whether the symptom pattern matches the DSM criteria. The question about thoughts of death or self-harm is highlighted regardless of the total.

What does the result mean?

A score of 10 or more suggests having the symptoms assessed by a professional. The questionnaire does not diagnose depression: that takes a conversation in which duration, course and other causes are examined, such as physical illness, grief, medication or bipolar disorder. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, please contact a professional or a crisis line straight away (findahelpline.com lists them by country).
