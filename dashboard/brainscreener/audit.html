About this test

What does the test measure?

The AUDIT is the World Health Organization's alcohol screening test. Its ten questions refer to the past twelve months and record three things: how much and how often you drink, signs of dependence (e.g. loss of control, drinking in the morning) and harm that has already occurred, such as guilt, memory gaps or injuries.

How is it scored?

Each question scores 0 to 4 points, and the total ranges from 0 to 40. The WHO bands: 0–7 low risk, 8–15 hazardous drinking, 16–19 harmful drinking, from 20 probable dependence. Scores of 5 to 7 are reported separately, because a lower threshold is recommended for women. Anyone who states that they never drink alcohol skips questions 2 to 8.

What does the result mean?

From 8 points a conversation with a professional about your drinking is worthwhile; from 16 an assessment is clearly indicated. The test is not a diagnosis and relies on your own statements — underestimating the amounts gives a score that is too low. With heavy daily drinking, alcohol should not be stopped abruptly without medical supervision, because withdrawal can be dangerous.
